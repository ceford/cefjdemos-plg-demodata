Honey is a sweet, viscous liquid food produced by bees from the nectar of flowers or plant secretions, which they store in hives for nourishment. Bees refine nectar through enzymatic activity and evaporation, creating a concentrated mixture of sugars (fructose and glucose), water, enzymes, and mineral nutrients. It is often used as a natural sweetener.

Key Aspects of Honey:

  • Production: Honey bees collect nectar, regurgitate it, and evaporate water from it within the hive to create a thick, stable food source.
  • Composition: Primarily glucose and fructose (~70 - 80%), with water, enzymes, minerals, and vitamins.
  • Varieties: Color, flavor, and aroma vary based on the floral source (e.g., clover, manuka).
  • Raw vs. Processed: Raw honey is strained but not pasteurized, keeping its natural enzymes and pollen. Processed honey is heated/filtered for a smoother texture.
  • Uses: Beyond food, it is used for its antibacterial properties, as a natural sweetener, and in skin care.
  • Storage: Due to its low moisture content and acidic pH, it does not spoil if stored properly.

Important Nutritional Note: Despite being natural, honey is considered a "free sugar" similar to refined sugar and should be consumed in moderation to avoid health risks like weight gain.
